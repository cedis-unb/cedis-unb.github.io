Brasilia, August 9, 2026

The student is an explorer. Knowledge is a continent with thematic regions; every deck read is a destination, every concept a star. The passport holds stamps, the diary holds memories, the compass suggests paths — but never dictates. That is the metaphor behind the **Atlas do Conhecimento** (Knowledge Atlas), presented today as part of the {{< link-interno "/projects/ecossistema_cedis" "CEDIS Ecosystem" >}}.

The Atlas is the student's personal space to see, revisit and celebrate their own learning journey. They arrive there from the {{< link-interno "/products/passport" "Learning Passport" >}}, through a secure handoff, and what they find is theirs alone. Progress is measured along **four dimensions** — exploration, practice, consolidation and reflection — without collapsing everything into a single score. On the `/mapa`, routes become territories and decks become destinations, with rings showing progress on each axis. The `/rotas` are thematic paths curated by the teacher; the `/carimbos` (stamps), twelve by default, never reset or expire. The `/diario` mixes automatic entries with reflections the student writes. The `/constelacao` draws the concepts already touched and, with relations coming from {{< link-interno "/products/concept_studio" "Concept Studio" >}}, links the stars to one another.

There is a `/bussola` (compass): a motivational profile based on the Octalysis model, explicit opt-in and switchable off at any time, showing the student which motivational drivers are most active in their trajectory. And there is `/proximos-passos` (next steps), plain-language recommendations produced by five transparent heuristics — no machine learning, no black box.

As important as what the Atlas does is what it refuses to do. It is not a learning management system: it hosts no content, grades nothing, issues no certificates. It has no ranking, no leaderboard, no public comparison between students. The teacher, for their part, uses a panel inside the {{< link-interno "/products/deckmanager" "Deck Manager" >}} where every aggregate is k-anonymous: no group of fewer than five students is ever shown as an absolute number. In the settings, the student exports all their data as a readable ZIP or unlinks the Passport — and the journey is erased in cascade.

The Atlas is in production at [atlas.cedis.tec.br](https://atlas.cedis.tec.br/), open source under the MIT licence, coordinated by Prof. {{< link-interno "/people/sergio_freitas" "Sergio Freitas" >}}. The {{< link-interno "/products/atlas" "Atlas do Conhecimento page" >}} details the features; the big picture is in {{< link-interno "/posts/news-post.035" "the CEDIS Ecosystem presentation" >}}.
