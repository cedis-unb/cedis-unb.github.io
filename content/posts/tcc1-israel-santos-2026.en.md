Brasília, July 2026

Large language models (LLMs) have become invisible infrastructure for both work and study in Software Engineering — but explaining how they actually work is rarely straightforward. **It is this double gap — motivation and structured teaching material — that Israel Thalles Dutra dos Santos chose to tackle in his TCC1**, presented at the Faculty of Sciences and Technologies in Engineering (FCTE), advised by Prof. {{< link-interno "/people/sergio_freitas" "Sergio Antônio Andrade de Freitas" >}}.

The proposal is a web platform aimed at teaching the fundamentals of LLMs — artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, and natural language processing — designed as an archaeological exploration journey. The metaphor is not decoration: each stage of the game corresponds to a content block the student excavates, with curiosity, unpredictability, and a sense of epic mission as narrative drivers.

The methodological backbone is the Octalysis framework. Israel administered the Octalysis questionnaire to a group of target students, analyzed the data with the Cronbach alpha coefficient, and identified four dominant *core drives*: development and accomplishment, epic meaning, empowerment of creativity and feedback, and unpredictability and curiosity. From those findings came ten {{< link-interno "/areas/gamification/" "gamification" >}} techniques selected for the platform — not chosen by taste, but grounded in the motivational profile actually observed.

The work brings together two fronts that often travel apart at CEDIS: {{< link-interno "/areas/ai_pln/" "artificial intelligence" >}} as the content to be taught and gamification as the instrument to teach it. TCC1 closes the foundations, requirements, and prototype phase — and sets the stage for TCC2, when the platform will be implemented and evaluated with real students.
