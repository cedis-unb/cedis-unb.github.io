A Harvard working paper by Fiona Chen and James Stratton (*Artificial Intelligence in the Firm: Bottlenecks in Software Production*, version of 28 October 2026) tracks 445 million work events from 720,542 professionals in 655 firms. After the adoption of coding agents, lines of code rose 44%, commits 36% and pull requests 35.7% — but the increases in resolved issues and completed epics are not statistically significant. The bottleneck moved to code review: time to approval rose 56.1%, time waiting for a reviewer 59.9%, and 82.8% of reviews are still done by people who already knew the codebase.

In this piece, Sergio Freitas reads the study alongside a video by staff engineer Vinícius Pasquantonio and the Software Engineering curriculum at the University of Brasília, and argues that the profession's axis is shifting from *writing more code* to *deciding, verifying, integrating and delivering*. Requirements, architecture, testing, software quality and configuration management gain centrality; so do critical thinking, technical communication and the responsibility for what gets merged. The piece was presented to about 150 students in two Compilers classes.
